## How to Send Real-Time Messages to Speakers During Events

Managing live events requires seamless communication between organizers and speakers. When a presenter is on stage, you cannot simply walk up and interrupt them. Yet situations arise constantly — a session is running over time, the schedule has shifted, a Q&A segment needs to begin, or an urgent announcement must be relayed. Chronograph.pro solves this challenge with its built-in message overlay system, allowing you to send real-time messages directly to the speaker's timer display without disrupting the audience experience.

## Why Real-Time Speaker Communication Matters

Every experienced event organizer knows the frustration of trying to signal a speaker from the back of the room. Hand gestures go unnoticed. Printed signs are easy to miss under stage lights. Walking on stage to whisper a message breaks the flow and looks unprofessional.

Real-time digital messaging changes this dynamic entirely. With Chronograph.pro, you can communicate with speakers discreetly and instantly from your dashboard — whether you are sitting in the control booth, backstage, or even managing the event remotely. The speaker sees your message overlaid on the same timer screen they are already watching, so there is no need to look at a separate device or break eye contact with their audience.

## How the Message Overlay System Works

Chronograph.pro's message overlay is built into the timer viewer — the full-screen display that speakers see on a monitor, tablet, or confidence screen positioned near the stage. When you send a message from the organizer dashboard, it appears as a prominent overlay on top of the running timer. The speaker can read the message at a glance and continue their presentation without interruption.

Key characteristics of the message overlay:

- **Instant delivery** — Messages appear on the speaker's viewer screen within seconds of being sent.
- **Visible without disruption** — The overlay is designed to be noticeable to the speaker while remaining unobtrusive to the audience if the timer display is positioned as a confidence monitor.
- **Temporary display** — Messages appear for a set duration, then automatically fade out so the timer returns to its normal view.
- **Works alongside the timer** — The timer continues running while the message is displayed, so the speaker never loses track of their remaining time.

## Step-by-Step Guide to Sending Messages

### Step 1: Open Your Room Dashboard

Log in to Chronograph.pro and navigate to the room where your event session is running. You should see the list of timers associated with that room.

### Step 2: Locate the Active Timer

Find the timer that is currently running for the speaker on stage. The active timer will show the countdown in progress.

### Step 3: Open the Message Feature

Look for the message or notification option associated with that timer. This allows you to compose a message that will be sent to the viewer screen linked to that timer.

### Step 4: Compose Your Message

Type your message in the text field. Keep it short and direct — the speaker needs to read it quickly while continuing to present. A good message is typically one line, no more than a brief sentence.

### Step 5: Send the Message

Click the send button. The message will immediately appear as an overlay on the speaker's timer viewer screen. The speaker can read it and acknowledge it without pausing their presentation.

### Step 6: Monitor and Follow Up if Needed

If the speaker does not seem to react or the situation requires additional communication, you can send a follow-up message. However, use restraint — multiple messages in quick succession can become distracting.

## Types of Messages You Can Send

The message overlay system supports any custom text, but here are the most common categories of messages that event organizers use:

### Time Warnings

- "5 minutes remaining"
- "2 minutes left — please begin wrapping up"
- "You are 3 minutes over time"

While the timer itself shows the countdown, a direct message adds a personal nudge that is harder to ignore than a number on a screen.

### Wrap-Up Requests

- "Please wrap up your session"
- "Next speaker is ready — please conclude"
- "Please finish within 1 minute"

These are the most frequently sent messages at conferences and corporate events where staying on schedule is critical.

### Q&A Transitions

- "Q&A session starting now"
- "Please transition to audience questions"
- "5 minutes for Q&A remaining"

When sessions include a question-and-answer segment, a clear message helps the speaker make a smooth transition.

### Schedule Change Notifications

- "Your session has been extended by 10 minutes"
- "Break moved to 3:30 PM — you have extra time"
- "Next session cancelled — you may continue"

Live events rarely go exactly as planned. The message overlay lets you communicate schedule adjustments in real time.

### Emergency and Urgent Notifications

- "Please pause — technical issue"
- "Fire alarm — please direct audience to exits"
- "Audio issue — please speak louder"

In critical situations, the message overlay becomes an essential safety communication tool.

### Custom Messages

- "Client in row 3 wants a demo after your talk"
- "Great energy — audience is loving it"
- "Reminder: do not mention Project X"

Any custom text can be sent, giving organizers full flexibility.

## How Speakers See Messages on the Viewer Screen

The speaker's view is the full-screen timer display — typically shown on a confidence monitor at the foot of the stage, a tablet on the podium, or a large screen visible only from the stage area. When a message is sent, here is what the speaker experiences:

1. **The message appears as an overlay** on top of the timer display. It is styled to be immediately noticeable — typically with a contrasting background or prominent text positioning.
2. **The timer continues running underneath**, so the speaker never loses sight of their remaining time.
3. **The message remains visible long enough to be read**, then automatically disappears after a set duration, returning the display to its normal timer-only view.
4. **No action is required from the speaker** — there is no need to dismiss the message, tap a button, or interact with the screen. They simply read it and continue.

This design ensures that the speaker can absorb the information with a quick glance, the same way they would check their remaining time, without needing to fumble with any device or break their presentation flow.

## Best Practices for Messaging Speakers

### Be Concise

Stage lights, presentation nerves, and audience engagement all compete for a speaker's attention. Your message should be readable in two seconds or less. Write short, clear, direct statements. Avoid lengthy explanations.

**Good:** "5 min left — please wrap up"

**Too long:** "Hi, just wanted to let you know that we are running a bit behind schedule and the next speaker needs to set up, so if you could start wrapping up in the next few minutes that would be great"

### Use Sparingly

Every message you send is a small interruption to the speaker's focus. If you send messages every minute, the speaker may start ignoring them — or worse, become visibly flustered on stage. Reserve messages for genuinely important communications.

A good rule of thumb: no more than two to three messages per session, unless there is an emergency.

### Establish Expectations Before the Event

Brief your speakers during the pre-event orientation. Let them know that:

- A timer will be visible on their confidence monitor.
- You may send occasional overlay messages for time warnings or important updates.
- They should glance at the timer periodically so they catch any messages.

When speakers know to expect messages, they handle them gracefully instead of being caught off guard.

### Time Your Messages Thoughtfully

Avoid sending messages during high-energy moments, emotional stories, or complex explanations. Look for natural pauses — transitions between slides, moments when the speaker takes a drink of water, or pauses between topic sections.

### Use a Consistent Tone

Keep your messages professional and neutral. Avoid sarcasm, humor that might be misread, or anything that could increase the speaker's stress. A calm, clear directive is always the safest choice.

### Coordinate with Your Team

If multiple organizers have access to the dashboard, designate one person as the primary communicator. Multiple people sending conflicting messages will confuse the speaker and undermine trust in the system.

## Use Cases

### Conference with Multiple Back-to-Back Sessions

At a multi-track conference, staying on schedule is paramount because delays cascade across all rooms. Use the message overlay to send time warnings at the five-minute and two-minute marks, and a firm wrap-up request if a speaker goes over. This keeps the entire event running smoothly without the awkwardness of physically approaching the stage.

### Corporate Town Hall with Executive Speakers

Senior executives often have packed schedules and may not watch the timer closely. A polite but clear overlay message such as "Board meeting at 2 PM — please wrap up" delivers the information they need without a visible interruption that the entire company would notice.

### Hybrid Events with Remote Coordination

When the event organizer is managing sessions remotely — perhaps coordinating across multiple locations or working from a production room — the message overlay is the only practical way to communicate with speakers on stage. There is no option to walk over or make eye contact; the digital message is your direct line.

### Panel Discussions and Moderated Sessions

During panel discussions, the moderator may need cues from the organizer: "Move to next question," "Wrap this topic," or "Audience mic is ready." Overlay messages provide these cues without the moderator needing to wear an earpiece or check a phone.

### Emergency Situations

If a fire alarm goes off, a medical emergency occurs in the audience, or a critical technical failure happens, you need to communicate with the speaker instantly. The message overlay provides an immediate, reliable channel: "Please pause and direct audience to nearest exits." Every second counts, and having a direct digital line to the speaker can make a significant difference.

### Schedule Adjustments Mid-Event

A previous session ran long, a keynote speaker arrived late, or a sponsor segment was cancelled. Whatever the reason, the schedule has changed, and the current speaker needs to know. Instead of causing confusion, send a clear message: "You have 10 extra minutes" or "Please shorten to 15 minutes — we are behind schedule."

## Tips for Effective Communication with Speakers During Events

1. **Pre-event rehearsal** — Show speakers the timer display and overlay during a tech check so they know exactly what to expect and where to look.

2. **Create a message template list** — Prepare standard messages in advance ("5 min remaining," "Please wrap up," "Q&A time") so you can send them quickly without typing under pressure.

3. **Position the confidence monitor strategically** — The best placement is at the speaker's natural eye level, just below or beside the audience. If the speaker cannot see the monitor easily, they will miss your messages.

4. **Assign a dedicated timer operator** — Have one team member whose primary responsibility is watching the schedule and sending messages. This person should not be multitasking with other event logistics.

5. **Log your messages** — Keep a record of what messages were sent and when. This is useful for post-event review and for improving your communication strategy at future events.

6. **Respect the speaker's autonomy** — The message overlay is a communication tool, not a command system. Speakers are professionals who will make judgment calls about when to wrap up a point. Trust them to respond appropriately to your messages.

7. **Test before the event** — Send a few test messages during setup to verify that the overlay is visible, readable, and disappears as expected. Adjust the confidence monitor brightness and angle if needed.

8. **Have a backup communication plan** — While the digital overlay is reliable, always have a fallback: a stage manager with a physical sign, a walkie-talkie to an assistant near the stage, or a predetermined hand signal. Technology should enhance your communication, not be your only option.
